Tomorrow's workout

A back-friendly session for tomorrow.

Train with supported positions, controlled ranges, and no need to push through back symptoms. The goal is comfortable, repeatable work—not testing the back.

45 minutes · Working sets at RPE 6/10 · About 4 good reps left in reserve

Safety first

Stop the exercise for sharp or increasing pain, pain travelling down a leg, new numbness or weakness, loss of coordination, a painful shoulder pinch, joint instability or giving way, dizziness, chest pain, or unusual shortness of breath. Do not work through symptoms that change your movement.

Keep every movement within a comfortable range. Reduce the range or load if back discomfort increases by more than 2 points out of 10, and stop if it does not settle promptly. Seek urgent medical help for new bowel or bladder changes, numbness around the saddle area, or rapidly worsening leg weakness.

Seek assessment from an appropriately qualified healthcare professional if symptoms are severe, persist, recur, or limit normal activity. This workout is general training guidance, not medical advice or a diagnosis.

0:00–7:00 — Readiness and warm-up

Before starting, record how the lower back, shoulder, neck, and ankle feel today. These numbers are context, not a pass/fail score.

  1. Brisk walk or easy march — 2 minutes. You should still be able to speak comfortably.
  2. Gentle neck turns — 5 each side, only through a comfortable range; do not force the stiff direction.
  3. Shoulder-blade squeezes and band pull-aparts — 8 slow reps of each, arms below painful range.
  4. Ankle rocks holding support — 8 each side, heel down.
  5. Supported sit-to-stands from a comfortable chair or box — 6 easy reps, using the hands as needed.

Readiness check: ordinary stiffness that settles as you move is useful context. If the warm-up makes symptoms clearly worse or changes your movement, scale back or stop.

7:00–33:00 — Strength benchmarks

Protocol for the first four exercises: take one easy rehearsal set, then choose a load for 6–8 crisp reps at RPE 6. Rest one minute, reduce the load by about 10%, and complete 8 controlled back-off reps. Keep the trunk comfortable and record both sets in the notes if their numbers differ.

Goblet box squat

7:00–12:00

What it trains: legs and trunk while the box gives you a repeatable depth for future comparisons.

How: hold one dumbbell at the chest, sit between the hips until you lightly touch the box, then drive the floor away. Keep the whole foot planted and knees tracking with the toes.

Guardrail: choose a box height that lets the ankle feel stable. Do not chase depth or bounce off the box.

Chest-supported dumbbell row

12:00–17:00

What it trains: upper-back and arm pulling strength without asking the neck or lower back to brace heavily.

How: lie chest-down on a slightly inclined bench, let the dumbbells hang, then pull the elbows toward the back pockets. Pause briefly and lower fully under control.

Guardrail: keep the neck long and shoulders away from the ears; stop before shrugging or twisting begins.

Floor glute bridge

17:00–22:00

What it trains: glutes and hip extension from a supported floor position, without a loaded standing hinge.

How: lie on your back with knees bent and feet planted. Gently brace, press through the feet, and lift the hips only as high as feels comfortable. Pause briefly, then lower slowly.

Guardrail: use bodyweight first. Keep the ribs relaxed and stop before the lower back arches or discomfort increases.

Neutral-grip dumbbell floor press

22:00–27:00

What it trains: pressing strength with the floor limiting shoulder extension and total range.

How: lie on the floor with palms facing each other, elbows about 30–45 degrees from the body, and press smoothly without forcing the shoulders forward.

Guardrail: use a modest load and stop for pinching or increasing shoulder pain. This is a tolerance benchmark, not a pressing contest.

Foot-assisted pull-up

27:00–33:00

What it trains: the exact vertical-pulling pattern needed for a future unassisted pull-up.

How: use a secure bar low enough to keep one or both feet on a box. Start with long arms, lightly push through the feet as needed, and pull the chest toward the bar. Complete 3 sets of 3–6 smooth reps.

Baseline: record reps plus the box height and whether one or two feet assisted you. Use the same setup next time. A securely anchored resistance band is optional, not required.

Guardrail: no uncontrolled hanging. If the overhead start position is uncomfortable, substitute an incline bodyweight row and record the bar height.

33:00–45:00 — Ankle, conditioning, and record

Single-leg balance + calf raise

5 minutes

How: stand beside support and time one stable single-leg hold per side, up to 30 seconds. Then hold support and perform one controlled set of calf raises per side, stopping at technical fatigue or 15 reps.

Record: balance seconds and calf-raise reps for each side. A fingertip save is fine; note it rather than pretending it did not happen.

Brisk conditioning

5 minutes

How: walk briskly, march, or use a light step-up rhythm. Set a pace where breathing is clearly elevated but you can still speak a full sentence.

Record: distance, step count, or the exact activity and pace, plus whether the talk test stayed comfortable.

Final 2 minutes: record the immediate lower-back, shoulder, neck, and ankle response. Add the next-morning response later. Do not repeat or progress an exercise that causes a meaningful delayed flare-up.
